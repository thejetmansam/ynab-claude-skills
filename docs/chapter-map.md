# Chapter map

The skills cite chapters ("per Rule 2 / Ch 3") rather than quoting text. This maps
each citation to what that chapter actually covers, so the references resolve whether
or not you have the book open.

**Source:** Jesse Mecham, *You Need a Budget: The Proven System for Breaking the
Paycheck-to-Paycheck Cycle, Getting Out of Debt, and Living the Life You Want*
(HarperBusiness, 2017).

The book is not in this repository and will not be. This is a structural index, not a
replacement. If you are going to run your money on this framework, buy it.

| Citation | Covers | Where the skills use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Intro** | The reset question. What do you want your money to do for you. | Mode 4 step 1, Mode 8 Rule Zero gate |
| **Ch 1** | The mindset shift. "Forget future money; use today's money to write your future." Why "can I afford it" is the wrong question. | Mode 5, the Decision Filter |
| **Ch 2** | **Rule One: Give Every Dollar a Job.** The priority hierarchy (immediate obligations, true expenses, highest priorities, just for fun). Question every assumption. The annual Question Everything exercise. The credit card rule and the float trap. Lifestyle creep. | Mode 2, Mode 3 tiering, Mode 8 Gate C, the float check in Mode 4 |
| **Ch 3** | **Rule Two: Embrace Your True Expenses.** The two camps of irregular expense. Turning a mountain into hills. Dream big, act small. Why Rule Two replaces the emergency fund. Variable income. | Mode 3 Tier 2 math, the missing-true-expense sweep in Mode 4 |
| **Ch 4** | **Rule Three: Roll with the Punches.** Changing the budget is not failing. Accountability to the bottom line rather than the line item. Being honest about patterns. Values versus priorities. | Mode 1 Rule 3 verdict, the grocery-target story behind Mode 3's underfunded flag |
| **Ch 5** | **Rule Four: Age Your Money.** Why age matters more than balance. The grain silo. The two paths: byproduct of Rules 1 to 3, or a deliberate sprint. Windfalls. | Mode 1 Rule 4 verdict, Mode 7 entirely |

| **Ch 9** | **When you feel like quitting.** The six pitfalls, each a form of self-inflicted perfectionism. The Budget Detox. The reset question again. | Mode 4 entirely |

## The business side

The business skill leans on Mike Michalowicz, *Profit First*, the same way and with the
same rule: paraphrase, cite, never quote at length.

**Edition matters.** These numbers follow the original 2014 edition. The revised and
expanded 2017 edition renumbers some material, most visibly by promoting the Income
account out of the advanced chapter into the core set. If a citation lands a chapter
off, that is why.

| Citation | Covers | Where the skills use it |
|---|---|---|
| **PF Ch 1** | *Taming The Beast.* Why the business eats everything it earns, and why more revenue on its own never fixes it. | The framing behind LIGHT mode's "challenge, do not flatter" |
| **PF Ch 2** | *How Profit First Works.* The flip, Sales minus Profit equals Expenses. The four core principles: small plates, serve sequentially, remove temptation, enforce a rhythm. | The allocation engine's sequencing |
| **PF Ch 3** | *The Naked Truth.* **The Instant Assessment.** Real Revenue (top line minus materials and subcontractors), the four buckets, Target Allocation Percentages by revenue band, and the gap against them. | DEEP mode step 2, cited explicitly in the skill |
| **PF Ch 4** | *Choose Your Own Adventure.* The foundational accounts, why physical separation beats intention, and setting your own starting TAPs per bucket from history rather than jumping to target. | The CAPs the runbook holds |
| **PF Ch 5** | *Day One, Quarter One, Year One and Forever.* **The twice-monthly allocation rhythm** (the book uses the 10th and 25th), **the quarterly distribution** (take 50% of the Profit Account, leave 50%), and **the ratchet**, roughly three percentage points a quarter, never backward. | The allocation engine entirely, plus the quarter-start extras |
| **PF Ch 6** | *Destroying Debt.* The Debt Freeze and the Debt Snowball. Keep taking profit while in debt; the distribution splits 99% to debt, 1% to you. | Not currently wired into a mode |
| **PF Ch 7** | *Found Money.* Profit comes from efficiency after the sale, not more sales. Firing the bottom client quartile. | DEEP mode step 3, the levers |
| **PF Ch 8** | *Sticking With It.* Accountability, and why execution rather than knowledge is the hard part. | The cadence argument in the runbook |
| **PF Ch 9** | *Advanced Techniques.* The Income account as a single deposit point, The Vault, and the other optional accounts. | Not currently wired into a mode |
| **PF Ch 10** | *Living Profit First.* Parkinson's Law by name, applied to personal cash as well as business cash. | The lifestyle-creep sweep on the personal side |
| **PF Ch 11** | *Where It All Falls Apart.* The four failure modes: going too big too fast, cutting the wrong costs, "plowback", and raiding the tax account. | The standing rules, especially never cutting owner pay |

The single most common failure in a Profit First implementation, and the one the
business skill checks for explicitly: **the quarterly distribution never actually
happens.** Money accumulates in the profit envelope, gets borrowed back for an
emergency, and the system quietly becomes a savings account with extra steps.
