# Profit First: Michalowicz, Full Book Distilled

The system distilled with chapter citations, paraphrased throughout with no verbatim
passages. `profit-first-index.md` is the one-page version.

**This is summary and commentary, not the book.** It is written so the
`ynab-profit-first` skill can cite a principle and move on. If the system is new to
you, buy the book: the case studies are most of what makes it stick.

---

## The One Idea

**The formula flip is the whole book.**

| | Formula |
|---|---|
| GAAP / conventional ("Frankenstein Formula") | Sales − Expenses = **Profit** |
| Profit First | Sales − **Profit** = Expenses |

The math is identical. Nothing logically changed. The entire leverage is *behavioral*: profit is taken **first**, off the top of every deposit, and the business is forced to run on what remains. Profit stops being leftovers (scraps, or an empty plate) and becomes the first portion served.

Michalowicz's thesis (Ch. 12, via Duhigg's *The Power of Habit*): habits run on autopilot, a trigger fires a routine, and changing them is genuinely hard. So the system does **not** try to change you. It puts guardrails around your existing behavior. "If logic worked, everyone would be rich… Leverage your emotions and behavior instead. Profit first. Always."

## Introduction & Chapter 1: Taming The Beast

### The two ways money problems happen
1. **Sales slow down**, the obvious failure. Living check-to-check, nothing covers the gap.
2. **Sales speed up**, the insidious one. Expenses rise to meet income immediately; incoming cash is lumpy; a drought arrives fast; and cutting back is nearly impossible because business + personal lifestyle are locked in, contracts make reversal costly, and **ego** makes you refuse to admit you over-built.

### Bank balance accounting (the default disease)
Nearly every entrepreneur manages cash by looking at the bank balance: big balance  euphoria  pay everything  zero balance  panic  sell/collect frantically. Owners check the bank account **daily** but rarely read the income statement, cash-flow statement, or balance sheet. This is human nature, not a character flaw, so the system must be **designed around it, not against it.**

### The Recency Effect
Humans over-weight the most recent experience. A fat deposit today makes the whole business feel healthy; a dry account makes it feel like a monster, same business, different day. It doesn't just color mood, it **dictates spending and selling decisions.**

### The Survival Trap
Point A = Crisis, Point B = Vision. In crisis, "just sell" feels like progress, but most fast-cash actions move you *away* from the vision. Three reasons it's a trap:
1. **Any sale feels like a good sale**, but you can't tell profitable income from debt-generating income while in crisis. (Ernie the lawn guy upsells gutter-cleaning  roofing  chimneys, buying tools and skills he doesn't have, each "easy" sale taking him farther from his actual business.)
2. **It's deceptive**, some fast-cash moves point at the vision by chance, so you conclude it's "clicking." It's randomness, not clarity.
3. **Attention collapses onto revenue**, all revenue looks equal, but some revenue costs far more in time and money than other revenue.

### GAAP is the Frankenstein Formula
GAAP's order, sales, then direct costs, employees, indirect costs, taxes, owners, and *finally* profit, has three flaws: (1) it runs against human nature (you spend whatever's available, with a good reason every time); (2) "what gets measured gets done," and it measures sales first, so profit and your own pay become afterthoughts; (3) it's complex enough to hide the truth (Enron posted profits while going bankrupt). GAAP's three reports (income statement, cash-flow statement, balance sheet) are still worth understanding, Profit First **plugs into** your accounting, it doesn't replace it.

**Action:** (1) draw a line in the sand, commit to a profitable business today; (2) don't beat yourself up about the past, self-recrimination is the only thing that actually blocks implementation.

## Chapter 2: How Profit First Works

The insight came from a PBS fitness expert: quick fixes fail; sustainable change comes from **lifestyle fixes that work with your nature and take effect before you can make a bad choice.** His first fix: **smaller plates.** Fill-the-plate-then-clean-it is unchangeable; plate *size* is trivial to change, and the unchanged habit then produces the desired result. (Research: US plate size grew 23% / 9.6"11.8" from 1900-2012; ~50 extra calories/day compounds to ~5 lb/year.)

### The Four Core Principles

| # | Principle | Business mechanic | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Use Small Plates** | Income hits the main account, then is immediately dispersed into separate accounts at preset percentages, each with one job | You spend what's on the plate; a smaller plate = smaller spend, habit intact |
| 2 | **Serve Sequentially** | ALWAYS move money in the fixed order: **Profit  Owner's Pay  Tax  Operating Expenses.** No exceptions | The vegetables (profit, pay, tax) get eaten first; if left for last they never get eaten |
| 3 | **Remove Temptation** | Move Profit and Tax out of arm's reach, a **second bank**, no online view, no checkbook, no debit card. "Deposit and forget it" | People act on convenience; inaccessible money can't be raided |
| 4 | **Enforce a Rhythm** | Do allocations + payables twice a month, on the **10th and 25th**, never ad hoc when cash piles up | A fixed cadence reveals patterns and defeats the Recency Effect |

**The load-bearing rule inside #2:** if there isn't enough left for Operating Expenses, that does **not** authorize raiding the other accounts. It means *you cannot afford those expenses and must cut them.*

### The four accounts (original edition)
| Account | Type | Where | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating Expenses | Checking (your existing one) | Primary bank | All income deposited here; bills paid from here |
| Owner's Pay | Checking | Primary bank | Owner salary |
| Tax | Savings (holding tray) | Primary bank | Temporary hold  swept to Bank 2 |
| Profit | Savings (holding tray) | Primary bank | Temporary hold  swept to Bank 2 |
| **Profit (no-temptation)** | Savings | **Second bank** | Where profit actually accumulates |
| **Tax (no-temptation)** | Savings | **Second bank** | Where tax actually accumulates |

Interest is not the point, holding + removing temptation is. Negotiate away bank fees/minimums or switch banks. (The 2017 edition adds a fifth **Income** account as the single deposit point; Ch. 9 below introduces it as the keystone "advanced" account.)

## Chapter 3: The Naked Truth

Stop asking **"How big is your business?"** (top-line thinking) and ask **"How healthy is it?"** Health is relative, judge by **percentages, not dollars** (a 6'8" 205-lb bikini model is at an ideal weight *for her height*). $500k yielding 80% in pay+profit is gorgeous; $50M yielding 2% is ugly.

### The Instant Assessment (15 steps, abbreviated)
Form columns: **ACTUAL | PF% | PF$ | THE BLEED | THE FIX.** Rows: Top Line Revenue, Material & Subs, Real Revenue, Profit, Owner's Pay, Tax, Operating Expenses.

1. **Top Line Revenue** = last 12 months, from your books.
2-4. **Material & Subs** = cost of materials (manufacturers/retailers) and/or subcontractors (autonomous, not on payroll, handle own taxes). Service firms whose own employees deliver the work: **n/a**. **Never** put your own employees' labor here.
5. **Real Revenue = Top Line − Material & Subs** (copy Top Line if n/a).
6. **Real Revenue is the number that matters**, it normalizes every business. (Real-estate agency: $5M billed − $4M agent commissions = a $1M business.)
7-10. **Actuals:** Profit = cash actually in the bank or distributed as a *bonus on top of* salary (if it isn't in the bank and wasn't distributed, you don't have a profit); Owner's Pay = regular payroll (not profit distributions); Tax = taxes paid + reserved; Operating Expenses = everything else.
11. **Reconcile:** the four categories sum to Real Revenue; Real Revenue + Material & Subs = Top Line.
12-13. **PF% / PF$:** pull target percentages from the TAP table for your Real Revenue band; multiply.
14. **THE BLEED = Actual − PF$.** Negative = you're bleeding out (under-funding). Typical pattern: negative on Profit/Owner's Pay/Tax, positive (excess) on OpEx.
15. **THE FIX:** "increase" where the Bleed is negative, "decrease" where positive.

**Real Revenue ≠ Gross Profit:** Real Revenue subtracts only materials & subs. Gross Profit also subtracts employee time. Profit First deliberately treats **every employee (full or part-time) as an operating cost, not cost of goods**, you pay them the same whether a job takes four hours or five.

### The TAP table: Target Allocation Percentages (Figure 4)

| Category | A · $0-250K | B · $250-500K | C · $500K-1M | D · $1M-5M | E · $5-10M | F · $10-50M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real Revenue | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| **Profit** | 5% | 10% | 15% | **10%** | 15% | 20% |
| **Owner's Pay** | 50% | 35% | 20% | 10% | 5% | 0% |
| **Tax** | 15% | 15% | 15% | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| **Operating Expenses** | 30% | 40% | 50% | 65% | 65% | 65% |

Notes: Profit dips at the $1M-5M band because that band demands the heaviest systems investment (OpEx jumps to 65%). Owner's Pay falls steadily as the owner shifts from only-employee to shareholder. **Tax is a flat 15% everywhere.** These are typical targets and a starting point, not gospel.

**Brand-new business:** don't wait for revenue, start at Profit **1%**, Owner's Pay **50%**, Tax **15%**, ratchet up quarterly; ignore advanced tactics until year one.

## Chapter 4: Choose Your Own Adventure (customizing TAPs)

Two failure modes to avoid: **analysis paralysis** and **going too big too fast** (jumping straight to 20% profit is "donating five gallons of blood at once"). TAPs are the *goal*; the Instant Assessment is the *start*. Bridge the two with many small quarterly steps.

- **Profit TAP**, three ways to set it: (1) benchmark ≥5 public companies in your industry (net income ÷ sales, averaged over 3-5 years); (2) your own best year *by percentage*; (3) easiest, pick a % off projected revenue.
- **Profit Account longevity rule of thumb** (if all sales stopped): **5% ≈ 3 weeks** of runway; **12% ≈ 2 months**; **24% ≈ 5 months.** As the % doubles, runway nearly triples (higher profit % = leaner business).
- **Owner's Pay TAP**, set it to what you'd pay your **replacement**, or **sum of working-owner draws × 1.25**. Don't martyr yourself by slashing your own pay to force the numbers.
- **Tax TAP**, 3-year lookback, accountant's estimate, bracket lookup, or **just use ~15%** (better to over-reserve and get a refund). The ~15% derives from: taxable portion (≈100%−OpEx%) × income-tax rate (~35%) ≈ 15-16%.
- **Operating Expenses % is always derived last** = 100% − Profit − Owner's Pay − Tax.

## Chapter 5: Day One, Quarter One, Year One and Forever

### Day One
1. **Tell your accountant.** If they dismiss Profit First, get a new accountant.
2. **Open the accounts,** and nickname each with its percentage in brackets so the ledger self-documents: e.g. `PROFIT [15%] *8843`, `TAXES [15%] *8839`, `OWNER PAY [25%] *8833`, `OPERATING EXPENSES [45%] *8812`.
3. **Start easy: historical % + 1%.** Never had a profit  start Profit at **1%**. Tax historically 5%  6%. Even if TAPs are far higher, begin with what you already do + 1%, so small you don't feel it. These are your **current allocation percentages (CAPs)**.
4. **Take profit today:** look at the current balance, subtract outstanding checks, split the remainder across accounts by your percentages. Then do it on every deposit going forward.
5. **Slice expenses to fund it, cut 10%, not 3%.** Bills don't vanish overnight, and you'll move another ~3% next quarter. Find the 10%: cancel anything not needed to run efficiently or keep customers happy, then negotiate every remaining expense **except payroll.** Be "frugal, not cheap."

### The 10/25 Rhythm (the twice-monthly ritual)
Taught by bookkeeper Debra Courtright: process money **only on the 10th and 25th** (or the prior business day). Chosen so payments land by the 15th and month-end. Seven steps, worked on $10,000 of deposits:
1. All revenue lands in the main account.
2. Compute allocations on the period's deposits by your CAPs.
3. Move fixed-dollar accounts (e.g. employee pay, petty cash) out.
4. **Sweep the full Tax and Profit balances to the second (no-temptation) bank.**
5. Pay yourself your set salary from Owner's Pay; leave the rest to accumulate.
6. Pay employees.
7. Pay bills from what remains in Operating Expenses.

If you can't fund all seven steps, the business is telling you it can't afford its current spending. Get into the rhythm anyway.

### Quarter One: the profit distribution
On the **first day of each quarter**, tally the Profit Account (excluding that day's allocation) and **take 50% as an owner distribution; leave 50%** as reserve. Split the distributed 50% among owners **by equity share.** Pay quarterly estimated taxes the same day.

**Hard rule: profit may NEVER go back into the business.** No "plowback," no "reinvest", those words disguise robbing Peter to pay Paul. The distribution must be spent on the owner **personally** (dinner, a couch, a vacation), that is what converts a cash-eating monster into a cash cow and trains the emotional reward.

### The quarterly ratchet: "one small step"
Each quarter, move your CAPs **closer to your TAPs, never backward.** Conservative default: **3 percentage points per quarter**, concentrated (Profit 58%) or spread (Tax +1, Profit +1, Owner's Pay +1).

### Year One / Forever
Year-end, finalize taxes: if you owe more than the Tax Account holds, this is the **one** time you may pull from the Profit Account for a non-distribution reason, then raise the Tax % and drop the Profit % by the same amount. Beyond the TAPs, push for **22%+ profit** or cut OpEx toward **15%.** Build a **3-month operating reserve** in the Profit Account; deploy the excess above that into growth or The Vault.

## Chapter 6: Destroying Debt

Keep taking profit **even while in debt**, waiting until debt is gone means you never build the efficiencies that eradicate it. Emotional key (via Suze Orman): derive **more joy from saving than from spending.**

- **The debt-distribution split:** while in debt, each quarterly profit distribution is **99% to debt, 1% to reward yourself.** The 1% keeps the habit and the emotional reward alive.
- **The Debt Freeze:** a rapid pay-down + a freeze on *new* debt. Print 12 months of statements; box all employee labor, highlight revenue-generating and keep-the-doors-open expenses, red-circle every recurring charge. Compute the monthly "nut" (year's expenses ÷ 12, excluding tax and owner pay). Cut to **10% below** your Instant Assessment target to guard against "expense bounce-back." **Rip off the Band-Aid, cut all at once**, not in dribs and drabs.
- **Kill recurring charges:** cancel automatic withdrawals; request **new credit-card numbers** so old auto-charges die; multiply every monthly bill × 12 to see its true cost. Renegotiate what's left: **open by asking for 25%, aim to land 10-15%**, and be genuinely ready to switch vendors.
- **The Debt Snowball** (Dave Ramsey): list debts **smallest to largest, ignoring interest rate**; pay minimums on all but the smallest; annihilate the smallest; roll its freed payment into the next. The non-negotiable across all methods: **you cannot add new debt while paying off old.**

## Chapter 7: Found Money (efficiency before growth)

**~95% of profitability comes from what happens "underground", after the sale**, not from selling more. (Idaho analogy: 95% of the state's water is underground.) Build efficiency **first**, then sell; more sales without efficiency just multiplies inefficiency.

- **Greg Crabtree:** there is **no ceiling on profit**, competitors copy any margin above ~20% and erode it, so you must keep expanding efficiency.
- **The big efficiency question:** "how do I get **2× the results with half the effort**?" Big questions yield big answers; "how do I shave two minutes" yields two minutes. (UPS minimized left turns  ~$6M/yr.)
- **Fire bad clients (Strategex study, 1,000 firms):** sort clients into 4 revenue quartiles. Top quartile = **89% of revenue and 150% of profit**; the two middle quartiles ≈ break-even; **the bottom quartile = 1% of revenue and a 50% profit LOSS.** Every quartile costs about the same to serve. Dump the bottom, start with one.
- **Clone your best clients**, similar needs = super-efficiency, better service, automatic referrals.
- **Selling more is the hardest way to raise profit**, best case percentages stay flat, worst (and common) case expenses rise faster than revenue.

## Chapter 8: Sticking With It

**The worst enemy of Profit First is you.** Left alone, people raid their own accounts. Knowing the system is 5% of the game; execution is 95%, and you get execution from **accountability.**

- **Accountability groups** work because shared pain is lighter, you honor commitments made to others, and a regular rhythm breaks big goals into milestones. Options: **Profit Accelerator Groups** (led by trained Profit First Professionals) or peer **Profit Pods** (no charging, no selling to each other).
- **Two-person minimum:** pick someone who wants profitability as badly as you, **not a business partner or anyone with a stake in your business.** Meet twice a month (~the 11th and 26th, right after allocations). Bring the **printed Profit Account statement** every time, numbers don't lie, which stops you cheating others *and* yourself.

## Chapter 9: Advanced Techniques

**Gate:** don't add advanced accounts until you've run **two full quarters** of the core system cleanly (allocating biweekly, amassing profit, taken distributions, in an accountability form, and *not breaking the rules*). More accounts = **simpler**, not more complex, an isolated balance per function forces better decisions.

### The advanced accounts
- **Income Account**, the keystone. ALL deposits land here; on the 10th/25th you allocate everything out to the core four. (Allocate Materials/Subs fixed amounts *before* running the percentages.) This is the fifth account of the 2017 model.
- **The Vault**, low-risk reserve; target a **3-month (one-quarter) buffer.** Write the access rules **in advance** (e.g. "if pulled for a sales drop, cut related costs within two months"). Its purpose is to force decisions *early*, not to buy time.
- **Stocking / Major Equipment**, save a fixed slice each allocation toward a known bulk purchase.
- **Pass-Through / Materials / Subcontractor / Commission**, for at-cost money that must NOT be allocated to Profit or Owner's Pay.
- **Employee Payroll**, predictable; fund it every 10th/25th; point the payroll service at *this* account.
- **Drip Account**, for retainers/pre-payments over a long horizon: park the lump sum, drip only the **earned** portion into Income (e.g. $120k for a year of work  $5k every 10th/25th).
- **Petty Cash**, a fixed biweekly amount with a debit card. "If it's not in Petty Cash, it ain't in my budget."
- **Sales Tax**, pull the tax portion out *first*; it's never your money.

### RIFA: Required Income For Allocation (replaces the "monthly nut")
The money you must deposit **per allocation period** to run healthy and pay yourself: **(monthly personal income need ÷ 2) ÷ (Owner's Pay %).** Example: $10k/mo need ÷ 2 = $5k; ÷ 0.31 = ~$16k needed each period  an instant, unambiguous sales target. Sum all owners' needs first if there are several.

### Other Ch. 9 tactics
- **The Pareto Overlap**, 20% of clients  80% of revenue; 80% of profit  20% of offerings. Connect the two axes: fire bad clients buying unprofitable items; up-sell profitable offerings to good clients.
- **Employee Formula**, each full-timer should generate **$150k-$250k Real Revenue** (a ~$1M company  4-6 people incl. you). Crabtree's labor test: Real Revenue ≥ **2.5×** labor (expensive/tech labor) or **4×** (cheap labor).
- **Mini power tactics**, rename the Tax account "The Government's Money"; hide all accounts but Operating Expenses on login; sweep PayPal/outside income into Income daily; kill the bank's "grand total" so the money isn't back on one big plate.

## Chapter 10: Living Profit First (personal finances)

Same machine for the household. Face the music (total all bills + debt), stop accruing debt, and pay yourself first every payday. Accounts: **Income · The Vault** (start = one month's rent/mortgage, different bank, no online/checkbook) **· Recurring Payments** (varying bills' monthly average +10%, plus fixed) **· Day-to-Day** (one per spender, each with a debit card) **· Debt Destroyer** (snowball).

- **Debt disbursement:** while in debt, **90%** of each quarterly distribution to debt, **10%** to enjoyment; after core debt, **45%** to accelerate mortgage/car, **55%** to yourself; once everything's owned outright, **100%** to you.
- **Lock in your lifestyle** (Parkinson's Law, spending expands to fill income): once you take a real salary, freeze lifestyle for **5 years.**
- **Wedge Theory** (Brian Tracy): **bank half of every income raise**, then take your standard savings % on top.

## Chapter 11: Where It All Falls Apart  (the highest-value chapter)

Root failure = **sliding back into old belief systems** ("it takes money to make money," "you'll make money at the end," "just sell more," "just follow GAAP"). The relief of fixing cash flow tempts you to revert to one-account, top-line thinking. The four named failure modes and their fixes:

1. **Going too big, too fast.** Allocating 20-30% profit on day one  can't cover bills next month  yank profit back  whole system defeated. **Fix:** start small, ratchet **1-2% per quarter.** (Jorge & Jose jumped to 20% and had to dial it back to the balance point.)
2. **Cutting the wrong costs.** Frugality zeal cuts *assets*, the things that create efficiency. (A knife company ran 1960s tools "to save money"; a rival made perfect knives at 4× the output per hour.) **Fix:** if a purchase raises the bottom line through real efficiency, keep it, find the same result cheaper, don't cut the capability.
3. **"Plowing back" and "reinvesting."** Dressing up raids on allocation accounts with fancy words. A credit-card bill is **debt, not an expense.** Too little in Operating Expenses is a **red flag that expenses are too high**, cut them, don't borrow.
4. **Raiding the Tax Account.** As profit grows the Tax Account over-reserves relative to last year's estimates, and the voice says "I'm over-reserving." **Reality:** estimates are based on the prior year and won't move mid-year; earn more, owe more. Never raid it, check with your accountant **every quarter** (taxes are trending up, which is a sign of health). Note "paying for your sins": you get the tax break on debt *interest*, not principal, so retiring debt out of profit is taxed.

**Action:** get real with a Profit First-trained accountant; schedule quarterly check-ins.

## Chapter 12: Financial Freedom Is a Few Clicks Away

The behavioral close. Profit First works **with you as you are** and puts guardrails around your humanness, no MBA, no self-reform required. You don't need a windfall or a miracle; put profit first and everything else falls in line. "Financial freedom is just a few small plates away."

## The numbers worth memorizing

- Allocations & payables on the **10th and 25th.**
- Tax TAP is **15% at every revenue band.**
- New business: **Profit 1% / Owner's Pay 50% / Tax 15%.**
- Quarterly ratchet: **~3 points/quarter, never backward.**
- Quarterly distribution: **50% to owner (by equity), 50% retained**, or **99%/1% (business debt)** and **90%/10% (personal debt)** while in debt.
- Profit runway: **5% ≈ 3 weeks · 12% ≈ 2 months · 24% ≈ 5 months.** Reserve target = **3 months.**
- Expense cut: **10% below** the Instant Assessment target; negotiate by asking **25%**, landing **10-15%.**
- Employee Formula: **$150k-$250k Real Revenue per head**; labor ≤ Real Revenue ÷ **2.5** (skilled) or ÷ **4** (cheap).
